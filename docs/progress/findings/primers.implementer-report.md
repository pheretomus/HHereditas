The primers module is finished: Tm, binding sites, design and hairpin/dimer checks. All 95 tests pass, my files typecheck with 0 errors, and Tm and hairpin/dimer results agree with primer3 to rounding error (far inside the 0.05 °C requirement). Nothing needed sudo, and no shared files had to change.

One thing needs a decision before release. `thal.ts` and `thal-tables.ts` reproduce primer3's hairpin/dimer engine exactly (rules, constants, evaluation order; parameters copied from its data files), so they should be treated as derived from primer3, which is GPL-2.0-or-later. The repository has no license yet. If the project will be permissively licensed (MIT/Apache), those two files need a legal review or a clean-room rewrite. The other files don't carry this concern.

**Agreement with the oracles** (largest difference seen)
- **Tm vs primer3 `calc_tm`:**
  - 2,566 random 8–60 nt cases × 3 salt corrections: 5.7e-14 °C.
  - 300 self-complementary, 150 cases of 2–7 nt, 300 cases above 60 nt: 0 to 5.7e-14 °C.
  - 900 DMSO/formamide cases: 0.
- **Tm vs Biopython** (600 cases): 4.6e-13 °C, both as a cross-check of 'santalucia' and as the reference for 'owczarzy2008'.
- **Hairpin/dimer vs primer3** (3,430 cases: 1,200 hairpin, 800 homodimer, 1,030 heterodimer, 400 end stability):
  - "Structure found" agrees in every case; Tm within 5.7e-14 °C, ΔG/ΔH within 3e-11 cal/mol.
  - The base pairs match primer3's drawn structure in all 2,697 cases that have one.
  - A live test with 180 freshly generated cases agrees as well.
- **Binding vs a brute-force Python reference:** 272 cases with 369 sites (both strands, 121 across the origin, 57 with mismatches, 131 with 5' tails), identical results.
- **Speed** (requirement: 50 primers × 10 kb under 50 ms, 1 primer on the genome under 1 s):
  - 50 primers on a 10 kb plasmid: 5 ms.
  - One primer on the 4.6 Mb E. coli genome: 12 ms (19 ms with 2 mismatches; 474 ms worst case when the 3' anchor is turned off).
  - Hairpin/dimer checks: 0.02–3.6 ms per call.

**Deliberate deviations**
- **GC function renamed to `oligoGcContent`.** `seq/stats.ts` (another agent) already exports `gcContent`, and both barrels at the package root would clash. The two also mean different things: mine counts ambiguity codes as their expected fraction ('GGNN' gives 0.75), theirs leaves them out (1.0).
- **'owczarzy' copies a primer3 bug on purpose.** primer3 drops a length term of the published Owczarzy 2008 equation (integer division in C). I reproduce that to match Primer3/Primer-BLAST, but it makes Tm of GC-rich oligos fall as Mg²⁺ rises above about 1 mM. I added 'owczarzy2008' with the published equation (checked against Biopython). The two differ by a median of +0.8 °C where Mg dominates, but by −8 to +21 °C at the edges of the condition range.
- **Ambiguity codes.** Degenerate primers are expanded instead of using primer3's averaged N parameters. `tm()` returns the mean over expansions, exact up to 4096 and sampled reproducibly above that (min/max may then be slightly off). A Tm is undefined below 2 nt, so `tm('')` and `tm('A')` return NaN rather than a number.
- **Empty input** to the hairpin/dimer checks returns "no structure"; primer3-py raises an error instead.
- **Small rules I chose where the spec was open:**
  - In binding, a mismatch only counts as annealed when the next two bases towards the 5' end match. With one base, random tail bases would often add a false mismatch to a perfect site.
  - The default 3' anchor is 5 bases.
  - Above `maxSites` the binding search throws rather than silently truncating.

**Known limitations**
- Binding-site Tm ignores mismatches, so it is an upper bound for mismatched sites.
- Hairpin/dimer checks: ambiguity codes never pair; at least one sequence must be 60 nt or shorter.
- The binding template must be uppercase, as stored in a record.
- Primer design picks the length only; it does not avoid hairpins or dimers.
- Above 60 nt, Tm uses primer3's GC% formula.
